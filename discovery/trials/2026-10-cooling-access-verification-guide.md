# Cooling Access Verification Guide

**Linked case:** [Cooling Access Is More Than a Map](2026-10-cooling-access-gap.md)  
**Status:** Prepared for human-led verification; not yet executed  
**Purpose:** Determine whether Lawrence County, Ohio has a clear, maintained source of truth for heat-event cooling resources—and whether a real coordination gap warrants any intervention.

## Ground rules

- This is an information-quality check, not a public safety advisory and not a substitute for official emergency instructions.
- Do not tell anyone that a facility is open, activated, or suitable until an authorized source confirms it for the relevant time.
- Do not publish personal contact information, visitor records, or sensitive details.
- Record what the source actually confirmed, the date/time, and any limits. A voicemail, unanswered call, old web page, or unverified third-party listing is **not** confirmation.
- No outreach has been made as part of preparing this guide.

## Human agency and privacy safeguards

These rules apply to every contact, note, and possible public-facing result:

- Keep the audit at the **facility, public-information, and process level**. Do not collect resident names, individual journeys, visit histories, health or disability status, household addresses, or lists of people presumed vulnerable.
- Participation is voluntary. State the purpose honestly, ask only role-appropriate questions, and accept “no” or “I don't know” without pressure.
- Do not record a staff member's personal details when an organizational role or public office contact is sufficient. Do not publish private correspondence or attribute comments by name without permission.
- Do not publish an address correction, operating status, eligibility condition, or “open now” claim until it is confirmed by a source authorized to speak to that fact. Show verification time and scope; leave unresolved conflicts visibly unresolved.
- Any resulting information should help people assess their options, not rank, track, compel, or automatically route them. Preserve alternatives and people's ability to decide for themselves.
- If the investigation begins to require sensitive personal data, individual-level tracking, or coercive control to make its proposed intervention work, stop and redesign the approach. Do not treat “public safety” as a blanket exemption.

## Suggested order of verification

Use public organizational contact channels and ask permission before treating any information as authoritative.

1. **Lawrence County Emergency Management** — ask which source is authoritative during an active heat event, who owns updates, and how the public is directed to current resources. The Ohio EMA county director list provides a public office contact. [1]
2. **Lawrence County office responsible for the cooling-center page** — identify the page owner and last verification date; reconcile any discrepancies with the provider.
3. **Briggs Lawrence County Public Library administration** — establish whether each branch is designated as a cooling resource during heat events, or merely has ordinary public hours; ask what “on standby” means in the separate warming directory.
4. **Ironton City Mission** — reconcile the two addresses appearing in public directories and verify event-specific hours, eligibility/access conditions, and how activation is announced.
5. **Local 2-1-1/referral channel** — ask how current cooling-resource information is maintained and how a caller is routed if transport is the barrier. Ohio Department of Health recommends 2-1-1 for cooling-center assistance/referrals, but that alone does not confirm local service coverage or hours. [2]
6. **Ironton-Lawrence County Community Action Organization** — ask when the next Summer Crisis Program application window is expected to be announced and whether its public information includes a reliable referral path for households needing immediate cooling. Do not assume the 2026 program window remains open. [3]

This is a suggested verification order, not a claim that any organization has agreed to participate.

## Existing referral and transportation pathway to verify

Ohio 211 says its 13 regional centers use a statewide database of public and private health and human-service agencies. Its public network and expansion pages confirm statewide coverage and identify United Way of Greater Cleveland as the provider extending service to Lawrence County. The provider's public resource-search page is branded for Cuyahoga County, but that branding alone does not show whether Lawrence County records are absent; the statewide system may be accessed through a separate navigator workflow. The real question is narrower: are the local cooling resources present, accurate, and operationally useful after the recent expansion? Ohio public-health guidance specifically points people seeking cooling-center help to 2-1-1. [2][4][6][7]

This is a process question for the provider, not a reason to create a competing directory. No 211 call or transport inquiry has been made.

The Community Action Organization's public transportation page describes Lawrence County Transit options, but the page may be stale. Do not promise a ride, quote a current fare, or assume a vehicle can be dispatched for a heat emergency without direct confirmation. [5]

Questions for the authorized 211/local referral role:

- Following the recent expansion, are Lawrence County cooling sites and emergency-management contacts present and current in the statewide resource database used by navigators?
- What process adds or updates Lawrence County resources, and who can submit corrections?
- For a Lawrence County cooling-related request, what current source do navigators consult?
- Can the referral distinguish a facility's normal public hours from heat-event activation and confirmed availability?
- If a site cannot be reached, what alternatives are actually offered?
- Can the caller be referred to a transport provider, and what current eligibility, cost, booking lead time, operating hours, and service area apply?
- Is there an established handoff from immediate cooling refuge to household utility or air-conditioning assistance?

Do not make a real person's situation the test case without their consent. A general process question is sufficient for this discovery stage. No 211 call or transport inquiry has been made.

## Opening script

“Hello. I'm checking the accuracy and coordination of publicly available heat-relief information for Lawrence County. I'm not asking you to disclose personal or client information. I want to make sure the public is directed to an authoritative, current source. Are you the right person to answer a few questions about how cooling resources are verified and updated?”

If not, ask which public office or role owns the information. Do not pressure staff to provide information outside their role.

## Core questions

### Source of truth and updates

1. Which webpage, phone line, dispatch process, or other source should residents and partner organizations trust during an active heat event?
2. Who has authority to designate or activate a cooling location?
3. Who is responsible for updating the public information, and what triggers an update?
4. When was the information last verified? Is there a review schedule?
5. If two public pages conflict, which process resolves the conflict and how quickly?

### Facility usability

For each listed location, where the respondent is authorized to answer:

1. Is this facility formally designated for heat-event cooling, or is it listed because it is normally open to the public?
2. What are normal hours versus heat-event hours? Are weekends and holidays handled differently?
3. What event or decision activates additional hours or services, and where is that status announced?
4. Are there material public-entry conditions or accessibility limitations that should be stated clearly?
5. Is transportation offered or coordinated? If not, what current referral should be given?
6. Who can confirm status on the day a person plans to travel?

### Prevention and referrals

1. Where should a household be referred for preventive cooling assistance, such as utility support or air-conditioner assistance?
2. Where can staff find the next official program dates and eligibility rules?
3. Is there a documented handoff between immediate cooling-refuge information and longer-term household cooling assistance, or are these separate pathways?

Do not ask for individual applicants' names, medical information, income details, or case histories.

Status meanings:
- **Verified:** directly confirmed by an authorized source, with timestamp and scope recorded.
- **Reported:** someone provided the information but their authority or the scope is not established.
- **Conflicting:** credible sources disagree and the conflict remains unresolved.
- **Unknown:** not checked, not answered, or no reliable evidence found.

## Decision rules

**Close as “existing process adequate”** if the responsible organizations identify a maintained source of truth, resolve the observed directory differences, and explain a workable path for residents needing transport or preventive assistance.

**Recommend a process/documentation change** if authoritative information exists but the public pages or referral handoffs are inconsistent. Prefer correcting the owner-maintained source or clarifying responsibilities over adding software.

**Consider a small bridge** only if accurate, authoritative status already exists but a demonstrated format/workflow mismatch prevents a useful downstream action. Define the user and acceptance test before implementation.

**Redirect the investigation** if the principal constraint is facility capacity, staffing, transportation, energy affordability, or eligibility rather than information. A better directory cannot solve a missing ride or an unavailable service.

**Stop** if the alleged mismatch is explained, no meaningful decision changes, or maintaining additional information has no clear owner.
