## Human flourishing and power check

Complete this before recommending an intervention; write “not applicable” only with a reason.

- **Agency gained or lost:** Does this expand people's understanding, choices, capabilities, and ability to act—or make them easier to monitor, direct, or control?
- **Who benefits / who bears risk:** Which groups gain, which carry the costs, and who has the least ability to refuse?
- **Dignity, rights, and access:** Could this exclude, stigmatize, coerce, surveil, manipulate, or treat people as disposable? Does it preserve meaningful choice and recourse?
- **Privacy and consent:** What personal data, if any, is truly necessary? Can the question be answered with less data, aggregate evidence, or voluntary participation?
- **Misuse and power concentration:** How could the capability be repurposed, who could control it, and what prevents harmful use?
- **Reversibility and accountability:** Can affected people challenge errors or opt out? Can the intervention be stopped or rolled back? Who is accountable?
- **Less intrusive alternatives:** Could an existing service, a process change, a voluntary tool, or no intervention deliver the benefit with less risk?
- **Boundary decision:** proceed / revise / reject, with reasons grounded in the canonical safeguards in `CONSTITUTION.md`.

A high projected benefit does not cancel a hard ethical boundary. If the intervention depends on coercion, pervasive surveillance, manipulation, domination, or denying people's dignity and agency, reject that mechanism and seek a genuinely different approach.

## Evidence hygiene

- Distinguish source claims from our interpretation.
- Prefer primary or authoritative sources for operational facts.
- Include counterevidence and credible alternatives.
- Mark missing or stale information as unknown; do not fill gaps with invented certainty.
- Never equate a plausible mechanism, a prototype, or a passing test with a real-world outcome.
- A rejected idea is still useful if the reason and evidence are preserved.
