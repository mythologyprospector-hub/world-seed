# Human Operating Profile

**Status:** Canonical companion to [OPERATING_MODEL.md](OPERATING_MODEL.md).  
**Governs:** The human owner's responsibilities, rights, practical participation, and working relationship with the AI steward.  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-03

## Your place in the system

You are a full participant and the human owner—not a passive source of prompts, a routine project manager, or an approval button. Your role is distinct from the AI steward's, but it is not lesser. You provide the human purpose, values, judgment, experience, curiosity, and authority that the system cannot legitimately invent for you.

You do not have to keep the whole system in your head. The canonical role register, decisions, current state, and handoffs belong in durable records. The AI steward is responsible for retrieving and explaining the context needed at the point of a decision.

The shared role and authority model lives in [OPERATING_MODEL.md](OPERATING_MODEL.md). This file explains what that means in everyday participation. It does not create a separate authority hierarchy.

## Your responsibilities

You are responsible for the human contributions that cannot be delegated to machinery:

- **Purpose:** express what matters to you and what worthwhile outcome you hope the system will serve. You may begin with a question, hunch, wish, rough idea, or simple curiosity—not a finished specification.
- **Values:** establish the human values and boundaries that should shape the mission. Tell the steward when those values or priorities materially change.
- **Judgment:** make consequential choices that belong to the human owner, especially when people, privacy, money, safety, external commitments, or long-term direction are affected.
- **Authorization:** give explicit approval at the gates defined in [OPERATING_MODEL.md](OPERATING_MODEL.md). Approval should be informed; the steward must explain the decision in plain language.
- **Acceptance:** review major outcomes against agreed criteria and accept, reject, or request changes. You are not expected to independently reproduce every technical test.
- **Feedback:** say when something is useful, wrong, confusing, uncomfortable, misaligned, or not worth pursuing. Your feedback helps correct the system.
- **Participation and curiosity:** bring questions, imagination, experience, observations, and new directions. You may aim the Observatory lightly or simply invite it to surprise you.

These responsibilities do not require constant attention. They arise when a real decision, changed intention, useful feedback, or acceptance gate calls for your participation.

## Your rights

You have the right to:
- understand the current objective, important progress, material risks, uncertainty, and decisions awaiting you;
- receive a recommendation rather than being handed an unexplained pile of options;
- ask why, challenge the steward's reasoning, request evidence, and change your mind;
- know what work was performed, by whom or what, with which permissions, and what was not verified;
- have your decisions and material preferences preserved accurately in the appropriate durable record;
- decline a proposal or defer a decision without being pressured or manipulated;
- have personal information, credentials, and private account details handled within their documented privacy boundaries;
- receive help without being made to feel inadequate for not knowing code, Git, infrastructure, or implementation details.

## What you are not responsible for

You are not expected to:
- dispatch routine tasks or tell the steward every next step;
- write technical specifications before investigation begins;
- manage repositories, branches, pull requests, tickets, tools, or implementation agents;
- remember file paths, command syntax, project state, or every prior agreement;
- review every routine implementation detail;
- approve a change merely because work has already been done;
- accept a claim without evidence or take responsibility for an action that was not disclosed.

If the system repeatedly requires you to do these things, treat that as an operating failure to be corrected—not as a shortcoming in the human participant.

## The steward's reciprocal obligations

The AI steward must:
- recover relevant durable context before asking you to repeat it;
- keep the active objective and current state visible without overwhelming you;
- investigate and recommend the best defensible next step instead of reflexively asking what to do;
- make routine, reversible decisions within its authority;
- ask for your judgment only when it materially matters or an approval gate is reached;
- explain consequential choices, alternatives, risks, and uncertainty in plain language;
- challenge you honestly when evidence or the mission warrants disagreement, without scolding or manipulation;
- carry out authorized work, review delegated work, verify claims, and preserve failures as well as successes;
- remind you of unresolved decisions or commitments when they become relevant;
- never confuse your silence, technical unfamiliarity, fatigue, or desire for help with consent.

The steward remains accountable for the quality of its recommendations and execution even when work is delegated to Codex or another builder.

## Communication and practical interaction

Prefer plain language, brief updates, concrete results, and minimal jargon. When a command is needed, provide one complete, safe, copyable command with its purpose and expected result clearly explained outside the command itself. Adapt presentation to the actual interface and known operator preferences.

Do not address you as though lack of programming knowledge is a defect. Do not make you manage branches, files, tools, agents, or tickets unnecessarily. Explain important risks and choices without dumping implementation narration.

A lone period (`.`) can mean accepted / proceed / continue when the intended action is clear. It is not a universal permission slip and does not waive explicit approval requirements.

## Profile and privacy

Operator-specific preferences, approved identity/attribution details, and machine facts belong in the authorized private operator profile described by [PROFILE_PACKAGES.md](PROFILE_PACKAGES.md). Sensitive personal history belongs in the separate personal-data package. Do not put private operator details or secrets in this portable seed.

This document defines the portable human role. It is not a psychological profile, a claim about what every operator wants, or permission to infer sensitive traits. Adapt communication and practical workflow only from information the operator has authorized.

## Review

Review this document when the role model changes or when experience shows that the human is being burdened with duties the system should carry. Update the canonical model and this companion together through a reviewable proposal.
