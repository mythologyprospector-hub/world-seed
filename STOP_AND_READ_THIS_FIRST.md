1. **If you aren't having fun, you're doing it wrong.**
2. **Always do it for the project's sake. Never pacify the human at the expense of the project.** Challenge ideas when evidence says they are wrong; protect the mission without taking authority that belongs to the human.

## REQUIRED ORDER OF OPERATIONS

### 1. Finish the factory

- Establish a coherent, privacy-safe foundation on the actual default branch.
- Resolve the pull-request stack deliberately; do not merge stacked drafts blindly.
- Keep portable machinery, operator configuration, personal records, and private account inventory in distinct boundaries.
- Make onboarding actionable for a non-programmer with a real machine and GitHub account.
- Make validators catch missing essential files, broken instructions, unsafe privacy boundaries, false claims, and untested recovery assumptions.

### 2. Prove the factory's capabilities

Record evidence for:
- fresh or isolated bootstrap;
- paginated repository discovery and reconciliation against an explicitly authoritative expected inventory;
- operator-profile continuity without depending on chat history;
- source/ref backup integrity and accessible GitHub-only records/settings;
- an isolated restoration drill for the exact scope claimed;
- a disposable blank-start project used only as a factory acceptance test;
- durable handoff to a new builder.

A passing test, export, backup file, green workflow, or written plan is useful evidence, but none alone proves complete account recovery or a working factory.

**Do not make perfect full-account backup a prerequisite when platform tier or permissions make it impossible.** Preserve and test the essential supported scope, enumerate unsupported or inaccessible categories and their consequences, record residual risk, and never claim to restore anything that cannot be restored. A bounded, evidenced recovery capability with honest limits is better than an impossible promise.

### 3. Preserve essential evidence, not every project

Maintain distinct records for:
- the observed account inventory; and
- the essential preservation inventory required to rebuild the factory.

Account-specific survivor names and inventory belong in authorized private recovery storage, not in this public seed. Before removing anything, glean and preserve unique lessons, history, data, settings, and operating records the factory needs. Require integrity checks, scope-appropriate restoration evidence, explicit documentation and acceptance of unrecoverable gaps, and human authorization for the specific destructive operation.

### 4. Rebuild only after the gates pass

The intended reset scope, the current account state, and the human authorization must be read from durable, authoritative sources. Do not delete or archive repositories merely because the factory-first mission is clear. Prefer reversible actions where practical. A successful reset is not proof of factory quality; the factory must demonstrate that it can rebuild reliably.

### 5. Judge results by evidence

Professional quality means appropriate architecture, maintainability, security and privacy, accessibility where relevant, useful documentation, automated quality gates, reproducible builds, honest claims, real user value, and evidence that the result works. Do not lower standards to flatter an operator or claim success before it is demonstrated.
